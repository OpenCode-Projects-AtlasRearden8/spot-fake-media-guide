# Glossary

Plain-English definitions, alphabetical. Where a term is covered in depth,
the chapter number follows.

**AI image (AI-generated image)**
A picture made by a generative model from a text description, rather than
by a camera pointed at the world. Chapter 02.

**AI detector**
Software claiming to decide whether media is AI-made. Currently unreliable
in both directions — flags real photos and misses new fakes — so treat it
as one signal, never a judge. Chapter 08.

**C2PA**
The main industry standard for **provenance** receipts attached to media
files (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity). Backed by camera
makers, software companies, and AI labs. Chapter 07.

**Compression**
The shrinking of a file for sharing; each re-save (a "re-encode") smooths
away detail. Heavy compression hides both real photo texture and fake
image tells — one reason verdicts stay humble. Chapter 05.

**Content Credentials**
The consumer-facing name for C2PA receipts: a signed trail inside a file
recording what made it and every edit since. Readable at
contentcredentials.org/verify. Chapter 07.

**Deepfake**
Media in which a person's face or voice is swapped or generated —
traditionally video; now used loosely for any synthetic-person media.
Chapter 05.

**EXIF / metadata**
Technical data stored inside a photo file (camera model, time, sometimes
location). Fragile — most platforms strip it — but a legitimate first
glance at where a file claims to have come from. Chapter 07.

**Keyframe**
A single well-chosen paused frame of a video — the unit you reverse-search,
since one frame usually leads back to the original clip. Chapter 06.

**"Leaked audio"**
A recording of unclear origin, usually spreading with a claim attached.
Treated in this guide as a claim to verify, not evidence. Chapter 04.

**Lip-sync tell**
The mismatch between mouth shapes and the sounds being made — the classic
deepfake weakness, easiest to catch on the closed-lip letters m, b, p.
Chapter 05.

**Multimodal AI**
A chat assistant that can work with more than text — images (and sometimes
audio/video) alongside words. The reason "ask AI to check AI" is possible.
Chapter 08.

**Provenance**
The receipt-trail of where a piece of media came from and what was done to
it. The quiet upside of this era: files can increasingly *prove* their own
history. Chapter 07.

**Re-encode**
A re-save of audio/video that changes file details (and usually quality).
Deepfakes love heavy re-encoding because it hides seams; humans should
respect it because it hides real texture too. Chapter 05.

**Reverse image search**
Searching the web *with* an image to find earlier versions of it and the
pages it lives on. The single most useful check in this guide. Chapter 06.

**Shallowfake**
Real media with a false story attached — wrong date, wrong place, wrong
cause. Not fabricated pixels; fabricated context. Chapters 01 and 05.

**Screenshot**
A picture of a screen. Convenient, common, and proof of nothing by itself,
since it's exactly as forgeable as any other image. Chapter 03.

**Stock photo**
A professionally made image licensed for anyone's use — the reason a
"grandmother" on a dating profile (see *The AI Scam Safety Guide*) can
appear on a luggage ad. Chapter 06.

**SynthID**
Google's invisible watermark, baked into pixels of content made by its AI
tools; one example of the wider approach of watermark-then-detect.
Chapter 07.

**Tell**
A repeatable clue that media might be fake — six fingers, a missing breath,
a pasted line's baseline. Two tells = suspicion; three = act. Throughout.

**Unresolved**
The legitimate verdict when checks come back ambiguous: not real, not
fake, not shared. Chapter 10.

**Voice cloning**
Generating new speech that sounds like a specific person, usually from
about thirty seconds of their recorded voice. Chapter 04.

**Watermark**
A mark inside media indicating its origin. Visible ones (platform labels)
and invisible ones (pixel patterns like SynthID) both exist — and both are
signals, not guarantees. Chapter 07.
