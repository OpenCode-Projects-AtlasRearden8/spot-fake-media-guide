# 09: The Family Group Chat (Where Fakes Travel Fast)

Every group chat is a small kindness economy, and fakes exploit it
ruthlessly. People forward because they care: from the aunt who wants
everyone to know to the cousin who sends "praying" clips. Nobody in the
chat is fooled because they're careless — they're fooled because the
forward button is two seconds wide and built for the feeling, not the
fact-check. This chapter protects the group chat without scolding anyone,
including you.

## The three-question pause (before forwarding)

1. **Do I even need to forward this?** This is the beautiful one, because
   the answer is nearly always no. The signal arrives; your heart is moved
   by it; you send love; the feed churns on. Most "amazing" media is a
   snack, not a statement, and the kindest thing to do with a snack is
   enjoy it, not archive it in all 47 of your chats.
2. **Where did it actually start?** Trace re-shares back to who *made* the
   thing. Forwards arrive stripped of authorship — and no author on a claim
   isn't privacy, it's subtraction of accountability. If nobody in the
   chain knows the origin, that's a legitimate stopping point: you're
   allowed to let an anonymous forward expire quietly.
3. **What would still be true if this were fake?** Warm awe, a quick
   laugh, righteous outrage — all of those survive being fake. But "my
   cousin is in the hospital," or "forward this to every relative before
   the vote," relies on truth. Spend your checking budget only on messages
   that change what people *do*.

## The forwarding hygiene habits (pick three; that's most of the protection)

- **Check before you forward.** Chapter 10's loop: trace, zoom, search,
  label. About ninety seconds, and it converts you into the family member
  with *the* reputation worth having.
- **Name the source when you share.** "From Dr. Okafor's clinic page,
  posted Monday" beats "trust me" by a mile — the extra clause costs
  nothing and saves everyone checking time.
- **When it's urgent, say "checking now" instead of forwarding blind.**
  "Wow — looking into this, one sec." You become the source-of-record
  instead of the source-of-spreading.
- **Own your corrections.** Forwarding is a fidelity promise, not a
  greatness promise; when something you sent turns out fake, a quick
  "oops, got me — ignore that one" keeps the economy strong (people trust
  *correctable* people).
- **Match the check to the stakes.** Life-or-death emergencies deserve the
  full loop; cute cat videos get a soft skim; there are no trophies for
  being the last family member to verify a meme.

## Scripts: ways to be kind while still being clear

:::note Script 1 — The gentle question (when someone else posts a fake)
"WOW — where's this from originally? I know reposts strip all context."
Then stop. No accusation; sincerity is invited. Half the time, the source
poster will *again* post something real, and you'll have converted an
argument into a habit.
:::

:::note Script 2 — The kind correction (when it's really, truly bogus)
"Turns out this is AI — I did a reverse image search and it started as a
concept-art post in 2024. Sharing the source link for whoever wants it: [link]"
:::

:::note Script 3 — The self-protective ask (the emotional carry without the exchange)
"I've been burned by fake forwards before — would you mind sending the
original link first? I check before re-sharing now; nothing personal."
:::

:::note Script 4 — The exhale (when you're too tired to check)
"Can't check this one right now — saving for later. PS if it's the one
everyone's been posting today, it might be the recycled one from 2024."
:::

## The two rooms you shouldn't have the conversation in

1. **Don't fact-check in a cheer-squad thread.** If the reply section is
   already a chain of "amen!", you're not going to win by launching
   statistics into an emotional stadium; politely correct the *original*
   poster in a DM so the correction lands close to the source, without the
   crowd.
2. **Don't do the deep-family-conversation in the group chat.** When it's
   one relative repeatedly sharing harmful media, that's a heart-to-heart
   matter — *The AI Scam Safety Guide*, chapter 09 ("The Family Friday
   Talk") carries that conversation-with-structure, including ground rules
   that keep everyone's dignity intact. A group chat is a kitchen, not a
   courtroom.

## The kitchen-table move worth teaching the whole family

Print the checklist (the last pages of this guide) and put it where you
talk — the pantry door, the fridge — and say, plainly: "New house rule,
everyone: if it moves you, check it, then share it. Takes one minute —
the card's right here." One minute is a fine price for a whole family
believing less and verifying more.
