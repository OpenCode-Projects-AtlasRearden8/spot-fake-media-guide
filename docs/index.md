# Start Here: Believe Less, Verify More

The camera used to be a witness. If a photo existed, something had happened.
Now an image can be generated in ten seconds, a voice can be borrowed, a
video star can be invented, and a screenshot can be faked in less time than
it took you to read this sentence.

This is a manual for checking what you see — a few small habits, a couple of
free tools, and a calmer relationship with your feed. No jargon. No fear.
Nothing you can't do on a phone, standing in line for coffee.

## The calm promise

This guide is **not** about distrusting everything. It is about spending
one to five minutes on the things that matter, and passing on everything
else. The motto fits on a sticky note:

> **Believe less, verify more.**

That doesn't mean become a cynic. It means that when something makes you
feel strongly — amazed, furious, touched, alarmed — that's your signal to
check *before* sharing, not after. And when the check comes back "I don't
know," that's allowed. "Unresolved" is a perfectly respectable verdict.

## What this guide covers

Everything you're likely to meet in a normal scrolling day:

- **AI-made images** — the gorgeous, the hilarious, the heartbreaking, and
  the "you won't believe" ones, plus the zoom-in tells that give them away
  (chapter 02).
- **Fake screenshots** — "receipts" that aren't, invented posts, doctored
  text threads, and how to read a screenshot for the seams (chapter 03).
- **Cloned voices and "leaked audio"** — voice notes, celebrity mash-ups,
  and mystery recordings, and how to listen past the smoothness
  (chapter 04).
- **Deepfake video** — the lip-sync problem, the hair-seam problem, why
  short blurry clips are where fakes hide, and the slow-motion trick
  (chapter 05).
- **Reverse image search** — the five-minute undo button everyone should
  learn; the single most useful check on this whole subject (chapter 06).
- **Provenance labels (C2PA)** — the quiet news: some content now travels
  with its own receipt, and you can read it (chapter 07).
- **Asking AI to check AI** — what a chatbot can genuinely spot for you,
  what it can't, and prompts that produce useful answers (chapter 08).
- **Family group chats** — where fakes travel fastest, and gentle habits
  and scripts that slow the spread without hurting anyone (chapter 09).
- **The calm playbook** — a five-minute loop, an "it's unresolved" verdict
  you're allowed to land on, and what to do with what you found
  (chapter 10).
- **Practice scenes, a printable checklist, and a glossary** — because
  reading about spotting is 20% of spotting; doing it is the rest.

## How this fits with *The AI Scam Safety Guide*

This guide is the **media side** of a two-guide set; *The AI Scam Safety
Guide* (from the same series) is the **money side**, and the two are
deliberately built not to repeat each other:

- **There:** scams aimed at you personally — AI-written scam messages,
  fake profiles and listings, cloned-voice *phone calls* begging for money,
  fake AI tool websites, too-good deals, verifying people and payments, and
  what to do if dollars already left the account.
- **Here:** fake *media* aimed at your beliefs — images that never
  happened, audio that was never said, video that was never filmed,
  screenshots that were never posted — wherever they show up: news,
  group chats, memes, and "ugh, look at THIS."

When a topic has deep roots over there — live phone calls and the family
passphrase live in that guide's chapter 04, payment "proof" screenshots in
its chapter 03 — this guide points at it rather than re-teaching. If the
fake media you spotted turns out to be aimed at your wallet, close this
manual and open that one.

## Who this is for

Anyone who scrolls. If you've ever forwarded an image because it *felt*
true, this guide is yours. If you've ever called a real photo fake and been
wrong, this guide is especially yours — one full theme in here is how to
avoid accusing real things of being fake, which causes its own quiet harm.

You need: a phone or a computer, a few minutes twice a week, and the
willingness to say "wait, let me look" instead of "I just sent it to you."
That's the whole kit.

## How to use it

Read 01–06 in one sitting first — that's the foundation and it takes about
half an hour. Then keep 10 (the playbook) and the printable checklist in
mind, and let the rest come from practice. Chapter 11 exists for exactly
that: five little mysteries you can solve with what you've learned, with
answers you can peek at — and a note on inventing your own practice sets,
which works even better than reading.

And one small favor to ask of you: the point of this manual isn't to make
you great at saying "FAKE." It's to make you the person in the group chat
who says "let me check" — calmly, kindly, and without making anyone feel
silly. People get fooled because these fakes are *good* and because humans
are wired to weep at things. Not because they're dumb.
